# Imaging and inspecting white print on clear PET film (lighting, background, optics, algorithms)

Reading notes for the report writer:

- Research date: 2026-11-17. Target case: opaque white letters (30-60 mm tall) plus one line of tiny white lot-code text on a clear, glossy, slightly wavy PET carrier, about 100 x 300 mm, one 5 MP colour GigE area camera (Hikrobot MV-CS050-10GC), 12 mm entocentric lens.
- Source quality tags used below: **[fetched]** = I read the page or PDF text. **[snippet]** = the page blocked automated fetching (HTTP 403/404/406) and the finding comes from the search-result summary only, so wording and numbers should be re-checked before they are quoted in a final report.
- Most lighting sources are vendor guides or marketing pages. They agree with each other on the physics, but none of them tests a white-on-clear heat-transfer label. Anything specific to that product is in "Inferences", not "Cited Findings".
- No prices were found for any option. Cost and complexity remarks are my own qualitative judgement and are placed under "Inferences".

## 1. Which illumination geometry shows which defect on transparent film

### Takeaway
No single geometry covers this label. A diffuse backlight gives the silhouette of the opaque print (layout, dimensions, missing or torn letters) and shows absorbing marks on the clear film, but it carries no surface information; scratches and dust need a dark-field view (low-angle front light or dark-field backlight), and marks on the white ink itself need a diffuse front light with glare kept under control (dome, off-axis diffuse, or crossed polarisers).

### Cited Findings

**Diffuse (bright-field) backlight**
- Opaque objects block the light and appear as a dark silhouette on a bright field, which is the basis for outline and dimension checks. Transparent objects transmit the light, so only their non-flat features appear dark, and contrast on transparent parts is usually low unless surfaces are sharply curved (example given: air bubbles in plastic). [fetched] — [Opto Engineering, Illumination geometries and techniques](https://www.opto-e.com/en/basics/illumination-geometries-and-techniques)
- Under a backlight the part appears only as a black silhouette, with no surface information; contours are ideal for metrology. [fetched] — [Vision-Doctor, Lighting for machine vision](https://www.vision-doctor.com/en/lighting/lighting-for-machine-vision.html)
- Transmitted light from behind shows foreign matter as a black silhouette (example: contamination in nonwoven fabric), and backlighting is not suited to detecting surface detail. [fetched] — [Keyence, Basics of lighting selection](https://www.keyence.com/products/vision/vision-sys/resources/vision-sys-resources/basics-of-lighting-selection.jsp)
- For transparent materials, backlighting tends to be a better option than front lighting, a diffuse source is generally needed because uneven light can look like a defect, and each case may need its own backlighting strategy. [snippet] — [Photonics Spectra, Material Affects Lighting Setup](https://photonics.com/Articles/Material_Affects_Lighting_Setup/a18868)

**Collimated / telecentric backlight**
- Collimated rays are parallel and are reflected much less from object edges than diffuse light; this removes the edge-reflection "border effect" on shiny round parts, gives accurate edges, and adds roughly 20-30 % depth of field with a telecentric lens. The page names shafts, screws, O-rings as applications and does not discuss films. [fetched] — [Opto Engineering, Illumination geometries and techniques](https://www.opto-e.com/en/basics/illumination-geometries-and-techniques)

**Dark-field front light (low-angle ring or bar)**
- With low-angle light, the specular reflection from a flat surface misses the lens and only light scattered by non-flat features (defects, scratches) is collected, so they appear bright on a dark background. Usually built with a low-angle ring light. [fetched] — [Opto Engineering, Illumination geometries and techniques](https://www.opto-e.com/en/basics/illumination-geometries-and-techniques)
- In dark field, lateral edges are very bright and scratches and dust are visible. [fetched] — [Vision-Doctor, Lighting for machine vision](https://www.vision-doctor.com/en/lighting/lighting-for-machine-vision.html)
- Basler's guide says shallow-angle light makes scratches appear as bright lines on a dark background, and that a lateral light source avoids shadows or reflections on transparent objects such as films. [snippet; page returned HTTP 406] — [Basler, Lighting techniques for machine vision](https://www.baslerweb.com/en-in/learning/illumination/)
- A glass-inspection patent states that scratches are normally not seen under bright-field illumination and that dark-field illumination is what reveals them; it combines both in one system. [snippet] — [US 6,437,357, Glass inspection system including bright field and dark field illumination](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6437357)
- One blog-style guide gives "typically under 15 degrees from the surface" for low-angle dark field. Low-authority source; treat the number as a rule of thumb only. [snippet] — [detectdefects blog, 2026](https://detectdefectsjekyll.pages.dev/blog/2026/03/22/machine-vision-lighting-complete-guide)

**Dark-field backlight**
- Ring or bar lights are placed behind a transparent sample so that the direct beam misses the lens; only light transmitted through the sample and scattered by non-flat features is collected, giving bright defects on a dark field. The sample must be transparent. [fetched] — [Opto Engineering, Illumination geometries and techniques](https://www.opto-e.com/en/basics/illumination-geometries-and-techniques)
- The Samsung smartphone cover-glass system (see section 6) supports both transmission and reflection dark-field layouts and reports that the transmission layout gave less parasitic glare on curved-edge samples. [fetched] — [Turko et al., Smartphone Glass Inspection System, ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)
- In web inspection, a particle lying on the surface and a particle embedded in a coating look the same in diffuse bright-field transmission; adding a near-dark-field directed light shows the disturbance of the coating around the embedded particle. The bright-field image gives the particle size, the near-dark-field image gives the size and severity of the disturbance. [fetched] — [Koenig (Schenk Vision), ISCST 2012](https://www.iscst.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/9-Schenk-Vision.pdf)

**Coaxial (on-axis) light**
- Light hits the surface perpendicular to the object plane; it is used for defects on highly reflective flat surfaces, and the degree of collimation sets the contrast. [fetched] — [Opto Engineering, Illumination geometries and techniques](https://www.opto-e.com/en/basics/illumination-geometries-and-techniques)
- For chips on a glass plate, Keyence recommends coaxial vertical light because the whole glass surface is lit uniformly, while ordinary reflected light scatters irregularly on the glass. It also calls diffuse on-axis light very useful for flaws on flat, shiny surfaces. [fetched] — [Keyence, Basics of lighting selection](https://www.keyence.com/products/vision/vision-sys/resources/vision-sys-resources/basics-of-lighting-selection.jsp)

**Dome / cloudy-day light**
- A dome lights the part from virtually every direction, like a cloudy day, which suppresses reflections and uneven illumination on curved or bumpy shiny objects. [fetched] — [Opto Engineering, Illumination geometries and techniques](https://www.opto-e.com/en/basics/illumination-geometries-and-techniques)
- Dome lighting suits shiny, bumpy surfaces but has to sit close to the target to work. [fetched] — [Keyence, Basics of lighting selection](https://www.keyence.com/products/vision/vision-sys/resources/vision-sys-resources/basics-of-lighting-selection.jsp)
- Keyence's own example of print on a chip seen through transparent film: ordinary lighting reflects on the film surface and causes halation; the recommended fix is diffuse light so that no specular reflection from the film reaches the camera. The same page reports higher print contrast through film with red than with blue light, attributed to higher transmittance and lower scattering. [fetched] — [Keyence, Basics of lighting selection](https://www.keyence.com/products/vision/vision-sys/resources/vision-sys-resources/basics-of-lighting-selection.jsp)

**Cross-polarised light**
- A linear polariser on the light and a second one on the lens, crossed at 90 degrees, reduces or removes hot spots from reflective objects; Edmund shows the ring-light-plus-lens arrangement specifically. An ideal polariser passes half of unpolarised light (I = I0/2), transmission through a second polariser follows Malus' law (I = I0 cos^2 theta), and real polarisers never reach exactly 0 % when crossed. [fetched] — [Edmund Optics, Introduction to Polarization](https://www.edmundoptics.eu/resources/application-notes/optics/introduction-to-polarization/)
- Stress makes glass and plastic birefringent. An unstressed clear object between crossed polarisers gives a completely dark field; stressed regions rotate the polarisation and show up as transmission variations. [fetched] — [Edmund Optics, Introduction to Polarization](https://www.edmundoptics.eu/resources/application-notes/optics/introduction-to-polarization/)
- Detecting internal stress (birefringence) in a transparent part needs a polarised backlight behind the part, which is a different setup from glare removal with front light. [snippet] — [VA Imaging, Polarization in your machine vision application](https://va-imaging.com/en-us/blogs/machine-vision-knowledge-center/polarization-in-your-machine-vision-application)
- A patent for oriented polymer sheets passes light through a first polariser, the sheet and a second polariser to reveal gel defects, i.e. polarised transmission is used as a defect contrast mechanism in oriented film, not only for glare. [snippet] — [US 5,666,199](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5666199)
- Polarisation cameras with on-chip micro-polariser arrays can suppress the strongly polarised glare component in software without physical crossed filters. [snippet] — [The Imaging Source, Polarization cameras in machine vision](https://www.theimagingsource.com/en-us/resource/glossary/polarization-cameras-in-machine-vision/)
- A 2025 paper images polariser film with Mueller-matrix (polarimetry basis parameter) imaging and reports clearly higher contrast for transparent defects such as bubbles and indentations, with a 99.3 % average detection rate using MobileNetV3-small. [snippet] — [Acta Optica Sinica 45(4), 0415001 (2025)](https://m.researching.cn/articles/OJ8c7bd35ae53e782f)

**Structured / pattern light and deflectometry**
- A vendor case study on transparent film describes transmitted-light deflectometry: a strip of light is sent through the film and material damage deflects or diffracts it, giving a signal; resolution down to 40 um is claimed. The page blocked fetching, so vendor name and camera type are unverified. [snippet] — [automate.org case study, Defect detection in transparent film material](https://www.automate.org/case-studies/defect-detection-in-transparent-film-material/boa)
- For polymeric polariser film, structured-light (stripe) illumination was proposed for transparent "aesthetic" defects that ordinary lighting does not show; about 200 defective samples were imaged and characterised (Lai et al., Polymer Testing 2017). A follow-up reports that extremely slight transparent defects are missed by both white light and plain structured light and are detected by using the edge of the light stripe. [snippet] — [Lai et al. 2017 record](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c417t-37202edbad4e83eb70baf98724a70ea9f510390fb35f6d97b277a8dc5c1c64283); [imaging-enhancement method record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4Obkqer7)
- Fringe patterns used as a diffuse source, with analysis of the modulation of the reflected pattern on a specular surface (Yue et al. 2019), can detect defects and contaminants but were not shown to tell them apart; cited as related work in the Samsung paper. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)
- Keyence describes LumiTrax on glossy targets as projecting a striped light pattern, capturing several images in quick succession and computing an image without glare and background pattern; it names glossy films, wrappers and blister packs. Marketing page without test data. [fetched] — [Keyence, Detecting hairline scratches and stains on glossy surfaces with LumiTrax](https://www.keyence.com/products/vision/resources/vision-resources/detecting-hairline-scratches-and-stains-on-glossy-surfaces-with-lumitrax.jsp)

**Line scan vs area scan**
- Continuous film and coated-web inspection is done with line-scan cameras. Schenk describes 8k-pixel line-scan cameras with pixel rates up to 320 MHz and, as an example, about 1.5/1000 inch resolution at 300 ft/min with four such cameras. [fetched] — [Koenig (Schenk Vision), ISCST 2012](https://www.iscst.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/9-Schenk-Vision.pdf)
- Fraunhofer FEP's roll-to-roll inspection system does 100 % web inspection with line-scan cameras, with optional sticky-roller web cleaning. [snippet] — [Fraunhofer FEP flyer](https://www.fep.fraunhofer.de/content/dam/fep/en/documents/Anlagenflyer/M07_Roll-to-roll%20inspection%20system_EN_net.pdf)

### Inferences
- Defect-to-geometry map for this label, derived from the cited physics (not tested on this product):

| Defect | Best first view | Physical reason |
|---|---|---|
| Layout / dimension errors, missing or torn letters | Diffuse backlight | Opaque ink blocks light, giving a high-contrast silhouette that does not depend on film gloss |
| Pen / ink marks and absorbing stains on the clear film | Diffuse backlight | Absorbers show dark on a bright field; nothing else in the clear area competes |
| Pen / ink marks and stains on the white letters | Diffuse front light (dome or off-axis diffuse, optionally cross-polarised) | White ink scatters diffusely, a dark mark absorbs; the backlight view carries no surface information |
| Scratches on the film, loose dust | Dark field (low-angle front over a black background, or dark-field backlight) | Scatterers send light into the lens; the smooth film sends specular light elsewhere |
| Scratches on the letters | Low-angle directional front light, ideally from more than one direction | A scratch in the ink is a relief feature; directional light gives shading, and a scratch through the ink also shows as a bright line under backlight |
| Pinholes, thin or semi-opaque ink | Diffuse backlight | Light leaks through missing or thin ink |
| Tiny lot-code text | Backlight if opaque enough, else front light on black | At about 0.17 mm/px the stroke width may be only a few pixels (see Gaps) |

- Transmission is far more tolerant of a wavy film than reflection. A thin, locally plane-parallel film barely changes the direction of a transmitted ray, whereas a surface tilt of angle a rotates the reflected ray by 2a. So waviness mainly hurts front-lit views (moving glare patches) and has little effect on a diffuse backlight view. This is basic optics, not taken from a cited source.
- A flat diffuse panel next to the lens or a coaxial light is a poor fit for a wavy glossy film at 350 mm: every local slope that points the mirror direction at the light produces a glare patch. A dome covers a much larger solid angle, so the reflection becomes a uniform veil instead of patches, but a dome must sit close to the part and a 300 mm long label needs a large one (or a flat-dome / tunnel type). Low-angle dark field avoids glare only as long as the film slope stays below roughly half the light's elevation angle.
- Cross-polarisation should remove the first-surface glare from the PET because that reflection keeps its polarisation, while light scattered by the white ink is depolarised and half of it passes the analyser. Expect a large light loss (at least a factor of about four overall with ideal polarisers: one half at the source polariser, one half of the depolarised return at the analyser). Biaxially oriented PET is strongly birefringent, so in a crossed-polariser transmission (backlight) arrangement the film itself will not be dark and will show colour or brightness patterns; crossed polarisers are therefore a glare-control tool for the front-lit shot here, not a backlight tool. The PET-birefringence statement comes from general knowledge and was not sourced in this session.
- Collimated or telecentric backlighting is aimed at edge accuracy on thick or round shiny parts. For a flat printed film the ink edge is already thin, so a diffuse LED panel is sufficient, and a collimated source of 100 x 300 mm would be large and costly.
- Deflectometry and stripe-pattern backlights are the tools that would show film waviness, dents and creases, but none of the listed defects for this label is a waviness defect, and a naturally wavy carrier would produce a strong nuisance signal. Lower priority for the demo.
- Qualitative cost/complexity ranking for a one-camera demo cell (my judgement, no prices found): diffuse LED backlight panel (low) < black matte background plus low-angle bar lights (low) < linear polariser film on lights plus lens polariser (low, but costs light) < large flat dome or tunnel light (medium) < multi-segment ring or bars with a sequencing controller (medium) < collimated backlight, deflectometry or line scan (high, also needs motion).

### Gaps
- No source tested these geometries on white ink on clear PET. All product-specific rows in the table above are reasoned from general physics.
- CCS, Advanced Illumination, Smart Vision Lights, Cognex and Stemmer lighting guides were not retrieved (several vendor and trade sites returned HTTP 403/406), so there is no direct citation from them for the geometry-by-geometry list.
- No quantitative data on how much film tilt a given dome, bar or ring geometry tolerates before glare appears.
- No source was found for the birefringence behaviour of PET heat-transfer carrier film specifically.
- Whether the lot-code strokes are resolvable at 0.17 mm/px was not researched; it depends on the actual stroke width.

## 2. Background under the film, and two-shot / multi-light schemes

### Takeaway
The sources point to a backlight as the "background" of choice for clear material, because it turns the unknown background into a uniform bright field, and to a second, front-lit or dark-field image for surface detail. Capturing several lighting states of the same part with one camera and a sequencing light controller is established practice (Schenk and OCS in web inspection, Keyence LumiTrax, Smart Vision Lights LLM, Advanced Illumination DCS quad controllers), although no source describes it for this exact label.

### Cited Findings
- Backlight gives the silhouette and edges but no surface detail; front or low-angle light is needed for surface features. [fetched] — [Keyence, Basics of lighting selection](https://www.keyence.com/products/vision/vision-sys/resources/vision-sys-resources/basics-of-lighting-selection.jsp); [Vision-Doctor](https://www.vision-doctor.com/en/lighting/lighting-for-machine-vision.html)
- A flexible-packaging printer wanted to stop printing a costly white ink layer whose purpose was to make transparent and translucent substrates inspectable. The inspection system it adopted has a top light for normal print, a "foil" light for special substrates and an active back light for transparent materials, configurable for substrate thickness and transparency. [snippet; page returned HTTP 403] — [Packaging Strategies](https://www.packagingstrategies.com/articles/99032-flexible-packaging-printer-seeks-inspection-of-substrates-without-using-white-ink)
- A patent on inspecting labelled containers images the label in both transmitted and incident light, and notes that infrared transmitted light gives high contrast between a hole in the label and the label around it. [snippet] — [US 8,581,977](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8581977)
- Schenk: scan-rate-synchronised light switching acquires the sheet under several illumination modes at the same location with a single bank of cameras, improving detection and classification without the cost of several camera banks. [fetched] — [Koenig (Schenk Vision), ISCST 2012](https://www.iscst.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/9-Schenk-Vision.pdf)
- OCS: dual-line cameras with LED lights enable "Multi Channel Evaluation", where the same defect is recorded by one camera under two lighting systems, for example transmission bright field and transmission dark field. [snippet] — [MacPlas article on OCS film inspection](https://macplas.it/en/node/12490)
- A steel-surface study sequenced the illuminations at the camera scan frequency to get four images of each defect, and found some scratches could only be told from dirt streaks under transverse dark-field light. Different material, same multi-channel principle. [snippet] — [stahleisen.de](https://www.stahleisen.de/?p=74208)
- Smart Vision Lights LLM (LED Light Manager): a program holds up to six sequences, each setting up to four lights (or four ring-light segments) to off, on, an intensity level or OverDrive strobe. On an event it fires the first sequence and the camera, waits for the exposure to finish, then fires the next. It has four LED output channels, two extra outputs (for example a camera trigger) and Ethernet. Aimed at photometric 3D and multispectral multi-light systems. [snippet] — [Fierce Electronics](https://www.fierceelectronics.com/embedded/light-manager-illuminates-multi-light-machine-vision-systems); [automate.org product news](https://www.automate.org/vision/news/smart-vision-lights-introduces-llm-led-light-manager-targeting-photometric-3d-multispectral-and-other-multi-light-machine-vision-systems/aph)
- Advanced Illumination DCS-400E / DCS-800E are multi-light "quad" controllers with sequenced strobing, marketed for computational imaging (photometric stereo, HDR, extended depth of field). [snippet; page returned HTTP 403] — [Vision Systems Design](https://vision-systems.com/lighting-optics/article/14180993/computational-imaging-lighting-controllers-dcs-400e-dcs-800e-released-by-advanced-illumination)
- Photometric stereo: successive images of a stationary object under a sequence of lights let software estimate the surface orientation at each pixel. [snippet] — [Quality Magazine, Creative illumination techniques enable computational imaging](https://www.qualitymag.com/articles/97261-creative-illumination-techniques-enable-computational-imaging)
- Keyence: Multi-Spectrum lighting combines high-speed capture with 8 LED colours; in LumiTrax mode the segments are fired separately to compute shape and texture images, and "text on film" is a listed application. Keyence states the lighting removes glare on films so that text and barcodes can be read stably. LumiTrax is tied to Keyence controllers (CV-X, XG-X, VS, VJ). [snippet for the first two pages, fetched for the third] — [Keyence Multi-spectrum lighting](https://www.keyence.com/ss/products/vision/vj/multi_s.jsp); [Keyence high-performance lighting guide](https://keyence.com/ss/products/vision/high-performance-lighting); [Keyence LumiTrax on glossy surfaces](https://www.keyence.com/products/vision/resources/vision-resources/detecting-hairline-scratches-and-stains-on-glossy-surfaces-with-lumitrax.jsp)
- Hikrobot's MV-LE light controllers come as MV-LE100 (analogue constant voltage), MV-LE200 (digital constant voltage, including 4-channel models) and MV-LE201 (digital constant current), with constant-on and strobe modes. I found no Hikrobot document that confirms built-in multi-channel sequencing on a single trigger. [snippet] — [DirectIndustry, Hikrobot MV-LE series](https://www.directindustry.com/prod/hikrobot/product-242099-2551192.html)
- The Samsung cover-glass system is a concrete multi-shot example with an area camera: 12 LED facets (6 opposite pairs) on a ring are switched in turn, the camera is triggered by the illuminator and takes one dark-field image per state, inside a light-absorbing box. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)

### Inferences
- Background comparison for front-lit imaging of white print on clear film (reasoned, not sourced):
  - **Black matte**: highest contrast for the white letters, and the right background for dark field (dust and scratches bright on black). Weakness: dark pen marks and stains on the clear film sit dark on dark and can vanish; the film's glare shows up strongly against black.
  - **White / light diffuse**: dark marks on the clear film become visible, but the white letters lose their contrast and the film casts faint shadows and double images of dust onto the surface below it.
  - **Mid-tone or saturated colour** (for example blue or green with the colour camera): white letters and dark marks both separate from the background in a single shot, by colour for the letters and by darkness for the marks. A workable single-shot compromise, but weaker for each defect than the dedicated views, and whatever dirt is on the coloured surface is imaged too.
  - **Backlight panel as the background**: replaces "whatever is under the film" with a uniform emitter. With the panel on, the result is the silhouette view. With the panel off its diffuser is a light grey surface, not a black one, so a dark-field shot over an unlit panel has less contrast than over black matte.
- A two-shot scheme therefore looks like the natural fit: shot A with the backlight on (layout, dimensions, missing or torn letters, absorbing marks on the clear film, pinholes), shot B with front or low-angle light and the backlight off (marks on the white letters, scratches, dust). Web-inspection practice (Schenk, OCS) and the transmitted-plus-incident label patent support the principle; I found nothing that calls it standard for discrete printed-film parts.
- For a stationary part in a demo cell no dedicated sequencer is needed in principle: the PC can switch two controller channels between two software-triggered frames. A sequencing controller matters when the part moves, because both frames must be taken within the time the part stays registered.
- Stains on white ink are a bright-field reflectance contrast problem (dark on white), so they need even diffuse front light and exposure set so the white ink is not saturated. Stains on clear film are a transmission contrast problem. These two needs pull the background choice in opposite directions, which is the underlying reason one image is not enough.
- If only one lighting state is allowed, a mid-tone coloured background with a large diffuse front light is probably the least bad single-shot option. This needs a bench test.

### Gaps
- No source directly compares black vs coloured vs backlit backgrounds for white print on clear film. The comparison above is reasoning only.
- No CCS or Cognex material on multi-light or photometric imaging was retrieved.
- Whether a Hikrobot MV-LE controller or the MV-CS050-10GC's own I/O can run an alternating two-light sequence was not confirmed; check the MV-LE200 manual and the camera's sequencer / line-output features.
- The Packaging Strategies item could not be opened, so the vendor and product name of the top / foil / back light system are unknown.

## 3. How commercial systems inspect transparent film, clear labels, glass, cover glass, DTF and heat-transfer products

### Takeaway
Documented commercial practice exists for continuous clear film (line scan, multiplexed bright-field and dark-field transmission) and for cover glass and optics (multi-direction dark field plus a CNN). For clear "no-label look" labels the public material is thin (UV light for clear ink, a paywalled two-camera case study). I found no documented automated vision solution for DTF transfer film or heat-transfer labels: DTF QC guidance is manual.

### Cited Findings
- **Clear film and coated web**: line-scan systems with multiplexed illumination, combining diffuse bright-field transmission with near-dark-field directed light; besides defects, the calibrated cameras map coating variation over 100 % of the web. [fetched] — [Koenig (Schenk Vision), ISCST 2012](https://www.iscst.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/9-Schenk-Vision.pdf)
- OCS describes typical transparent-film defect classes such as gels / specks (looking like a water drop in the film) and black specks, recorded in transmission bright field and transmission dark field by one dual-line camera. [snippet] — [MacPlas article on OCS](https://macplas.it/en/node/12490)
- Transmitted-light deflectometry for transparent film, claimed down to 40 um. [snippet] — [automate.org case study](https://www.automate.org/case-studies/defect-detection-in-transparent-film-material/boa)
- **Cover glass**: Samsung R&D built a system with area colour cameras, 35 mm lenses, a 12-facet switchable ring (each facet 3 x 3 white 3 W LEDs with about 25 degree FWHM), a light-absorbing enclosure and an XY stage for zone-by-zone capture, with a U-Net-style CNN that segments scratches from the stack of dark-field images, followed by DBSCAN to group pixels. Scratches typically cover less than 0.1 % of the sample area, which drove special loss design. The authors cite ISO 14997:2017 as proposing white-light dark field for surface imperfections of optical elements. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)
- The same paper surveys commercial optics inspection tools (Dioptic ARGOS, written "AGROS" in the paper, and RedLux OptiLux SD): both use dark field with dome-shaped LED illuminators and morphological analysis, and according to the authors have limits in separating defects from contamination. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)
- A separate study on glass screens classified images with YOLOv3 to separate fine scratches from dust and stains. [snippet] — [Development of machine vision system for off-line inspection of fine defects on glass screen surface (IEEE record)](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_ieee_primary_9826789)
- **Clear labels**: barcodes printed in clear ink on labels have minimal contrast; Banner's answer is a UV ring light (365 or 395 nm) on its iVu reader so the ink fluoresces. [snippet] — [Banner Engineering, barcode verification on clear labels](https://www.bannerengineering.com/us/en/solutions/track-and-trace/industrial-bar-code-verification-on-clear-labels.html)
- A CI-Vision case study for L'Oréal describes a two-camera system inspecting transparent "no-label look" labels on shampoo and conditioner bottles and verifying the date / lot code near the label. The article is paywalled past the summary, so the lighting is not known. [snippet] — [Pharmaceutical Online](https://www.pharmaceuticalonline.com/doc/vision-inspection-enables-zero-defects-from-0001)

## 4. Holding the film flat, and what non-flatness costs in dimension accuracy

### Takeaway
With an entocentric lens the size error is proportional to height change divided by object distance, and a point's position error is its distance from the optical axis times that ratio, largest at the image corners. At 350 mm that is about 0.29 % per mm of lift, or roughly 0.43 mm (about 2.5 px at 0.17 mm/px) at the end of a centred 300 mm label per mm of lift. A telecentric lens would cut this by one to two orders of magnitude but needs a front element at least as large as the label, so flattening the film is the realistic fix.

### Cited Findings
- Opto Engineering gives the entocentric relation dH = (ds / s) x H, with a worked example: 12 mm lens, 1/3 inch detector, 20 mm object at 200 mm, 1 mm displacement gives a 0.1 mm size change. With a telecentric lens the same displacement changes the dimension by 0.0017 mm; the magnification error is reduced to 1/10 to 1/100 of a standard lens. Good telecentric lenses have a telecentric slope of about 0.1 degree (0.0017 rad). [fetched] — [Opto Engineering, Telecentric lenses tutorial](https://opto-e.com/resources/telecentric-lenses-tutorial)
- The front lens of a telecentric lens must be at least as large as the object field diagonal (stated elsewhere in the tutorial as the object's largest dimension), which makes these lenses larger, heavier and more expensive. The tutorial also uses "object thickness more than 1/10 of the FOV diagonal" as a guideline for when telecentric optics pay off. [fetched] — [Opto Engineering, Telecentric lenses tutorial](https://opto-e.com/resources/telecentric-lenses-tutorial)
- Vision-Doctor's calculator (inputs: sensor size, pixel count, focal length, working distance, +/- height variation) reports the error for the image corners; the error is zero at the image centre and largest in the corners. The page states that telecentric lenses are essential for accurate measurement and have much lower distortion. No formula or worked example is shown. [fetched] — [Vision-Doctor, measurement error from height variation](https://www.vision-doctor.com/en/service-en/sen-t1/vision-system-calculations/measurement-error-height-variation.html)
- Counterpoint: for detection (as opposed to measurement) the perspective shift is a fraction of a millimetre, and the advice is to compare that shift with the tolerance before deciding on telecentric optics; telecentric lenses also have a fixed working distance. [snippet] — [Overview.ai, Telecentric lens in machine vision](https://www.overview.ai/blog/telecentric-lens-machine-vision/)
- Vacuum tables for film exist as lab accessories: the KRUSS ST3210 uses a porous table top to pull films and soft samples flat for measurement. [snippet] — [KRUSS ST3210](https://www.kruss-scientific.com/en/products-services/accessories/st3210)
- Newton rings come from interference between light reflected at two smooth surfaces in near contact; anti-Newton-ring sheets prevent them by keeping a small gap through a micro-rough layer, and the patent states the effect is insufficient when the mean particle diameter in that layer is below 0.4 um. [snippet] — [US 7,244,490, Anti-Newton ring sheet](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7244490)
- Static on film lines causes curl, dust attraction and wrinkles; bar ionisers are the usual remedy. [snippet] — [Keyence, static control for film and paper](https://www.keyence.com/products/static/industries/film-and-paper.jsp)

### Inferences
- Worked numbers for this cell, using the cited relation (position shift = radial distance from the optical axis x height / object distance):

| Object distance | Scale change per 1 mm lift | Shift at the end of a centred 300 mm label (r = 150 mm) | Error on the full 300 mm length if the whole label lifts 1 mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 350 mm | 0.29 % | 0.43 mm (about 2.5 px at 0.17 mm/px) | 0.86 mm |
| 600 mm | 0.17 % | 0.25 mm (about 1.5 px at 0.17 mm/px) | 0.50 mm |

  "Object distance" means distance from the lens entrance pupil, which is not exactly the mechanical working distance.
- **The stated geometry looks inconsistent and should be checked.** If the MV-CS050-10GC has 3.45 um pixels (2448 x 2048, 2/3 inch class sensor; I did not verify the datasheet in this session), a 12 mm lens at 350 mm gives about 0.10 mm/px and a field of about 245 x 205 mm, which does not contain a 300 mm label. A scale of 0.17 mm/px with a 12 mm lens corresponds to an object distance of about 600 mm and a field of about 420 x 350 mm. Both rows are given above for that reason.
- A wave or curl of 0.5 to 1 mm near the label ends therefore costs roughly 1 to 2.5 px of position error per end, in the same range as or larger than the edge-location precision. If dimension tolerances are tighter than about +/- 0.5 mm, flatness has to be controlled or measured.
- Local waviness also shortens the projected length (chord vs arc), but for small slopes this is second order compared with the perspective term.
- Telecentric optics for a 300 mm long part need a front element of more than 300 mm. That is far outside a small demo budget and volume; this is a judgement, as no price was found.
- Practical flattening options and their side effects (reasoned, not sourced for machine vision):
  - **Vacuum table**: flattens without adding optical surfaces. Conflicts with a backlight unless the plate is translucent or perforated, and holes or pores show through the clear film in both backlit and front-lit images.
  - **Glass cover plate**: flattens well and works with a backlight, but adds two more reflecting surfaces (more glare, ghost reflections), collects dust and scratches of its own that dark field will show, and can produce Newton rings where the film touches the glass. Anti-Newton or anti-reflection glass reduces the rings, but its micro-texture scatters light in a dark-field shot.
  - **Electrostatic hold-down**: flattens thin film on a plate but attracts dust, which works against the dust problem.
  - **Edge clamping or light tension, or simply a longer object distance**: tension flattens curl along one axis; doubling the object distance halves the perspective error at the price of resolution or a longer lens.
  - **Software**: calibrate the plane with a board at the film plane, and measure centre-to-centre and relative spacings near the image centre where the error is smallest; place critical dimensions under the optical axis.

### Gaps
- No machine-vision source was found on vacuum, glass-plate or electrostatic hold-down specifically for inspecting printed clear film, so the trade-offs above are not backed by citations.
- No measured flatness figure for the actual labels (how many mm of wave or curl) is available; the error table needs that number.
- The camera pixel size and the real object distance need to be confirmed against the datasheet and the bench setup.
- No price or catalogue data for telecentric lenses covering a 300 mm field was collected.

## 5. Academic work on anomaly detection for transparent objects and films

### Takeaway
MVTec AD 2 is the main recent benchmark that includes transparent and backlit or dark-field scenes, and it shows current unsupervised methods are weak there and that several of them degrade sharply when lighting changes. In the film and glass literature the consistent message is that the imaging method (polarimetric, structured light, multi-direction dark field) decides whether a defect is visible at all, and ordinary CNN detectors work once the contrast exists.

### Cited Findings
- MVTec AD 2 has eight scenarios and more than 8,000 high-resolution images, and was built to include transparent and overlapping objects, dark-field and backlight illumination, and test sets with lighting changes to assess robustness. The abstract states that state-of-the-art methods remain below 60 % average AU-PRO. [fetched abstract] — [Heckler-Kram et al., arXiv:2503.21622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21622); journal version [IJCV, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-026-02743-0)
- Category and lighting list as extracted from the arXiv HTML: Can (reflective, diffuse bright-field front light), Fabric (diffuse front), Fruit Jelly (semi-transparent, back light plus front light), Rice (diffuse front), Sheet Metal (reflective, directed dark-field front light), Vial (transparent with clear liquid, diffuse bright-field back light), Wall Plugs and Walnuts (bulk goods, diffuse front). [fetched; extracted by a summariser, check against the paper's table] — [arXiv:2503.21622v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21622v1)
- Test design: each scene has regular, under- and overexposed images, plus additional light sources (spotlights or a second ring light) that create reflections on Can and Vial and uneven lighting on the bulk scenes. TEST_priv uses the training lighting; TEST_priv,mix mixes seen and unseen lighting. [fetched; same caveat] — [arXiv:2503.21622v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21622v1)
- Mean results at 256 x 256 input, AU-PRO at FPR 0.05, TEST_priv then TEST_priv,mix: PatchCore 28.8 / 26.0, RD 26.4 / 25.0, RD++ 27.1 / 25.2, EfficientAD 30.8 / 19.2, MSFlow 24.3 / 11.9, SimpleNet 21.1 / 13.7, DSR 20.3 / 17.4. All methods are below 31 % on TEST_priv. [fetched; same caveat] — [arXiv:2503.21622v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21622v1)
- Mean pixel-level segmentation F1, TEST_priv then TEST_priv,mix: PatchCore 3.7 / 3.4, RD 18.1 / 16.7, RD++ 19.2 / 16.7, EfficientAD 15.4 / 8.0, MSFlow 21.8 / 9.0, SimpleNet 17.7 / 8.7, DSR 10.9 / 9.5. The authors point out that PatchCore's AU-PRO is near the leaders while its thresholded F1 is very low, so threshold-free metrics alone overstate practical usability. [fetched; same caveat] — [arXiv:2503.21622v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21622v1)
- Lighting sensitivity from those numbers: reverse-distillation methods lose about 1 to 2 points under mixed lighting, while EfficientAD loses 11.6 and MSFlow 12.4 AU-PRO points. [fetched; same caveat] — [arXiv:2503.21622v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21622v1)
- Conflicting figure: a later arXiv paper quotes average AU-PRO_0.05 of 58.7 % for EfficientAD and 53.8 % for PatchCore on MVTec AD 2, which does not match the sub-31 % values above. Probably a different FPR limit or input resolution, but the snippet does not say. [snippet] — [arXiv:2505.19750v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19750v2); contradicted by [arXiv:2503.21622v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21622v1)
- Polariser / optical film: structured-light imaging for transparent aesthetic defects (Lai et al. 2017, about 200 defect samples); edge-of-stripe imaging for extremely slight transparent defects; "saturated" structured-light imaging combined with confocal measurements to build a labelled set; a lightweight polariser defect detection network (DDN, J. Intelligent Manufacturing 2020) motivated by speed and memory limits; Faster R-CNN applied to polariser images (2018); Mueller-matrix imaging with MobileNetV3-small at 99.3 % average detection (2025). [snippet for all] — [Lai et al. 2017](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c417t-37202edbad4e83eb70baf98724a70ea9f510390fb35f6d97b277a8dc5c1c64283); [imaging-enhancement method](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4Obkqer7); [DDN](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10845-020-01536-7); [Faster R-CNN polariser, JIH-MSP 2018](https://bit.kuas.edu.tw/2018/vol9/Number6/JIH-MSP-2018-11-006.pdf); [Acta Optica Sinica 2025](https://m.researching.cn/articles/OJ8c7bd35ae53e782f)
- Cover glass: multi-direction dark-field stack fed to a U-Net-like segmentation network with a joint segmentation-plus-reconstruction loss for extreme class imbalance and "nested" convolution kernels against overfitting. The authors argue that handcrafted and classical ML pipelines lacked accuracy and flexibility and that their runtime grows with the number of defects. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)
- Earlier glass and optics work summarised there: Tao et al. 2015 combined line-scan dark field with a second microscopic bright-field pass for classification (slow; weak scratches barely visible in bright field); Tao et al. 2016 used dark field only, with an air dust / fibre remover and morphological features to separate stain, scratch and residual dust; Wang et al. 2019 used double-threshold segmentation plus shape features and a binary-tree classifier. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)

### Inferences
- For this label the benchmark evidence argues against relying on an off-the-shelf unsupervised anomaly detector over an uncontrolled background: even on purpose-built backlit and dark-field scenes the best mean AU-PRO_0.05 is about 31 %, and feature-embedding / student-teacher methods with fast inference (EfficientAD) and flow models (MSFlow) are the ones that fall most under lighting shifts. Reverse-distillation models were the most lighting-stable in that table, PatchCore in between.
- Fixing lighting and exposure (as the project already did with a fixed exposure) is therefore not a detail: it removes the distribution shift that the benchmark shows to be damaging.
- A label with known artwork is a more constrained problem than generic anomaly detection. With a backlit silhouette, template or golden-image comparison after registration can cover layout, missing or torn letters and absorbing marks, and learned models can be kept for the surface classes (scratch vs dust vs stain) where the film literature also ends up using CNN classifiers or segmenters.
- A stack of images under different lighting states can be fed to a network as channels, as in the cover-glass paper. That is a direct template for a backlit plus dark-field pair.

### Gaps
- No public dataset of printed transparent film, clear labels or DTF transfers was found. The polariser-film datasets in the papers above do not appear to be published.
- Per-category MVTec AD 2 results for the transparent Vial and Fruit Jelly scenes were not extracted, so I cannot say whether transparent scenes are harder than the rest within that benchmark.
- The MVTec AD 2 numbers above were pulled from the arXiv v1 HTML by an automated summariser; check them against the paper's tables (and the IJCV version) before quoting. The 58.7 / 53.8 % conflict is unresolved.
- No transparent-object-specific findings from newer foundation-model or zero-shot anomaly detectors were researched.
- The MDPI Applied Sciences 10(1):385 paper that surfaced in the polariser search was not opened; its content is unknown.

## 6. Dust versus real defects

### Takeaway
Systems avoid dust rejects in two ways that are usually combined: remove the dust before imaging (ionised air knife, sticky rollers, static control) and discriminate optically, because a scratch scatters directionally (its brightness changes strongly with illumination azimuth) while dust and stains scatter roughly evenly, and surface particles light up in grazing or dark-field channels where embedded or absorbing defects do not.

### Cited Findings
- Measured basis for multi-angle discrimination: rotating the illuminator in 2 degree steps, scratches show sharp intensity peaks versus illumination direction, while dust and stains show weak modulation and are often saturated. For the common triangular-profile scratch, brightness is highest when the light direction is perpendicular to the scratch. The system uses this higher brightness variation as the key feature for telling scratches from other signals. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)
- The same paper notes that even human inspectors struggle to tell real defects from contaminants and resort to wiping, which can itself create new defects. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)
- Tao et al. 2016 (as summarised there) paired dark-field inspection with an air dust / fibre remover and used morphological features to separate stain, scratch and residual dust. [fetched] — [Turko et al., ICAART 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/102233/102233.pdf)
- A patent on separating particulate contamination from defects uses a glancing-angle "dust" channel: dust sitting on the surface scatters strongly at high angles and appears as high-intensity regions, while a scratch is essentially not visible in that channel. [snippet] — [US 12,175,654](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12175654)
- Schenk: a surface particle and an embedded particle look alike in diffuse bright-field transmission; adding near-dark-field directed light separates them. [fetched] — [Koenig (Schenk Vision), ISCST 2012](https://www.iscst.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/9-Schenk-Vision.pdf)
- EXAIR case: a lens maker's vision system had about a 20 % failure-to-read rate because of dust and flash; ionised air knives triggered by part presence removed most of it. A second EXAIR note reports an ionised air curtain eliminating false rejects on a video inspection station. Vendor case studies, no independent data. [snippet] — [EXAIR application note](https://www.exair.com/knowledgebase/applicationsearch/keeping-dust-and-debris-off-a-visual-inspection-system.html?p=6); [EXAIR blog](https://blog.exair.com/2015/02/03/static-eliminator-helps-with-video-inspection/)
- On film lines static leads to dust attraction (as well as curl and wrinkles); bar-type ionisers are used for static removal across film and web. [snippet] — [Keyence, static control for film and paper](https://www.keyence.com/products/static/industries/film-and-paper.jsp)
- Roll-to-roll inspection lines offer web cleaning with sticky rollers ahead of the cameras. [snippet] — [Fraunhofer FEP flyer](https://www.fep.fraunhofer.de/content/dam/fep/en/documents/Anlagenflyer/M07_Roll-to-roll%20inspection%20system_EN_net.pdf)
- YOLOv3 was used to separate fine scratches from dust and stains on glass screens, i.e. learned classification on top of the imaging. [snippet] — [IEEE record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_ieee_primary_9826789)
- In DTF practice a dust speck on the film is itself a quality problem because it can leave a blank dot in the transferred print, and operators use a magnifier to decide whether a white spot is dust or design. [snippet] — [PerfecPress DTF QC guide](https://perfecpresshtv.com/blogs/news/dtf-transfer-film-quality-control-and-testing-methods)

### Inferences
- Whether loose dust is a defect has to be decided with the customer first. The DTF guide suggests dust on the transfer side can matter for the final print, in which case "dust" is a class to report, not to suppress.
- Cheapest effective measures for a demo cell, in order: an ionising blower or ionised air knife just before the camera, and a cover over the cell to keep dust off the background or backlight surface; a peelable PET film charges easily, so static control addresses the cause, not only the symptom.
- Optical discrimination available with one area camera and switchable lights:
  - Backlit vs dark-field pair: a signal that is bright in dark field but leaves no dark spot under backlight is a small scatterer (dust, fine scratch); a signal that is dark under backlight is an absorber (ink, stain) or a large particle.
  - Two or more dark-field azimuths (for example bar lights along the long and the short side, fired separately): strong change between azimuths indicates a scratch, similar brightness indicates dust or a stain. This follows the Samsung result directly; that system used 6 direction pairs, and how well 2 directions work is untested.
  - Shape: elongated, thin, straight or gently curved signals vs compact blobs; fibres are the hard case because they are elongated like scratches.
- A clear film is seen through, so dust on the underside, on the background or on a cover glass appears in the image as well. A black matte background or a backlight diffuser placed some distance below the film plane lets depth of field blur dust that is not on the film; this is reasoning, not a sourced technique.
- A re-inspect step (blow off, image again, reject only if the signal is still at the same place) is a simple way to separate loose dust from fixed defects in a manual-load demo. I did not find a source describing it.

### Gaps
- No independent, quantitative data on how much ionised air or sticky rollers reduce false rejects for printed film.
- No source quantifies dust / scratch separation with only two or four illumination directions; the cited system used twelve facets and a trained CNN.
- No information on the customer's acceptance criteria for dust on heat-transfer labels (size, count, which side).
- The dust-channel patent and the EXAIR notes were read only as search summaries.
